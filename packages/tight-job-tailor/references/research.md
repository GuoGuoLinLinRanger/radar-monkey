# Research

Every claim about the company traces to a page you fetched, with a date. Snippets are leads. Stop early only when two good sources in a row change nothing.

## The employer, in order

1. **Their own material**: product, docs, engineering blog (fetch recent posts and any that name a system from the posting), repos. Pull out what they sell and to whom, stage and headcount, the stack as they describe it, the team this seat sits on.
2. **Careers page and everything it links to**: "how we work," principles, handbook, "what we look for," "how we hire," interview guides, founder letters. Fetch in full; they often say outright what the company wants. Testable norms ("we ship weekly," "design docs reviewed in writing") move the profile; slogans do not.
3. **Other current postings**: sentences shared across postings are the company's bar; bullets unique to this one are the manager's. The count of similar openings says growth or backfill.
4. **Interview reports** ("[company] [role] interview process [year]"): what they test is what they value.
5. **The manager or team's writing and talks**: what they are proud of, what they struggle with.
6. **News tied to the seat**: funding and its stated use, launches, layoffs, reorgs. Why does this seat exist now?
7. **Comparable postings elsewhere**: to separate role-family boilerplate from this posting's specifics.
8. **Employee commentary**: texture on pace and screening only; never a trait on its own.

Query starters: `[company] engineering blog`, `[company] careers`, `[company] how we hire`, `[company] funding 2026`, `[company] github`, `[company] [role] interview process`, `[title] [comparable company] job`. Confirm the domain when the name is common. Never search for the candidate as a person.

Record: `[title](url) (YYYY-MM)` beside each claim; both sides of any disagreement, with which is more recent and first-party; what could not be found. "Likely" and "probably" must trace to a stated reason.

## Who reads first

| Company | First reader | Verifies in seconds | Reads as strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early-stage startup | Founder or hiring manager, every application | Things built and shipped by this person, fast | Shipped work with a link, 0→1 ownership, breadth, direct user contact; brand matters less than founders admit |
| Growth-stage | In-house recruiter with the manager's scorecard, then the manager | Stack overlap stated exactly; scale numbers | Current-stack match plus operating at the next order of magnitude; "preferred" often names what the team runs |
| Big tech / large public | Sourcer or recruiter, often behind an AI ranking layer | Title, employers, dates, first bullet of the latest role | Brand employers, progression, scope numbers, the posting's own vocabulary |
| Mid-size with an HR generalist | Non-technical screener following the manager literally | Years stated, tools named as in the posting, a matching title | Explicit, nothing inferred |
| Enterprise / regulated / government | HR and compliance gates, then a technical lead | Degree, credential, clearance, years, continuity | Credentials at the top; process vocabulary; startup vocabulary reads as risk |
| Research lab | Researchers | Venues and authorship position | Papers, citations, open-source adoption |

Volume: big tech and enterprise see hundreds per posting; early-stage and mid-size often tens. High volume raises the value of what a first pass can verify. A 3,000-person private company screens like big tech; a 30-person company with a large round has growth stakes but early-stage screening.

**Early-career** (new grad, intern): education, internship substance, projects with users, selection results carry the page; a skills list is search vocabulary. **Experienced** (~3+ years): work history dominates, the latest role and its first bullet are the product, education shrinks to a line. At "2–4 years," treat as experienced.

A few facts: the first look is a sort lasting seconds (titles, employers, dates, first bullet); bullets are second-pass evidence, and both passes matter. Human screeners notice keyword stuffing. Content-based auto-rejection is rare; the real auto-reject is the knockout question, and at large employers AI ranking *orders* the queue, so the posting's vocabulary and quantified outcomes affect where a resume lands. Gaps are filtered by some HR-led employers and ignored by others; never misstate dates.

## The seats

Separate from the employer research, a few fetches per experience and project on the resume — commented-out seats included, since a hidden seat may be the one this posting wants — so that reaches are about this seat and not a generic one: the user's own live URLs (project sites, repos, a personal site; the user's public artifacts, not the person), the employer's engineering blog or docs for the product area the points imply, any link the user left in a comment. Pull out what the product does and for whom, the scale it runs at, the stack the team is public about, what a person with this title on this team plausibly touched. This research makes assumptions specific; it never invents a fact about the user.
